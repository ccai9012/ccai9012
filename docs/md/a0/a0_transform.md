## 6. TRANSFORM

***Move from testing AI to working with AI.***

After identifying how the model behaves, use selected artefacts as starting points for a more open-ended **human–AI design exploration**.

The objective now changes. Rather than keeping the model uninformed, you may deliberately provide context, explain your intentions, introduce references, refine prompts, correct the model, and use other tools introduced in tutorials.

The aim is not simply to generate more alternatives, but to develop a **coherent transformation strategy through iteration**.

### 6.1. Develop selected collections

- Select at least **3 collections** from your earlier investigations.
- For each collection, define a clear **transformation intention**: what are you trying to change, improve, explore, or challenge?
- Produce approximately **10 variations per collection**.
- Develop the alternatives as part of a coherent investigation rather than as unrelated generations.
- Maintain enough continuity with the starting artefact that the transformation can be understood and compared.

### 6.2. Explore possible transformations

The appropriate transformation will depend on your chosen artefact. For example:

| Artefact category | Possible transformations |
| --- | --- |
| **Bamboo scaffold** | Alter bracing; connections; individual members; supports; spacing; overall configuration. |
| **Floor plan** | Modify circulation; room relationships; openings; unit organisation; core relationships; massing. |
| **Façade / building** | Vary openings; pattern; articulation; shading elements; material expression; massing. |
| **Interior / place** | Modify lighting; materiality; furniture; enclosure; spatial organisation; atmosphere. |
| **Frame / truss** | Alter topology; members; joints; supports; proportions; overall configuration. |

These are starting points rather than fixed requirements. Your transformation should respond to the particular characteristics, problems, or opportunities you identified in the original artefact.

### 6.3. Work iteratively

For each selected collection:

- **Begin separately:** Treat each collection as its own design investigation.
- **Provide context:** You may now explain the artefact, your intentions, constraints, relevant observations, or other information that could help the AI.
- **Guide the process:** Refine prompts; provide references; identify successful or unsuccessful features; correct misunderstandings; ask for targeted revisions.
- **Use different tools:** You may combine conversational AI with image generation, image editing, coding, computational tools, or other methods introduced in tutorials where useful.
- **Build on previous outputs:** Use promising results as inputs for subsequent transformations rather than repeatedly restarting from the original artefact.
- **Document the process:** Record the starting artefact; transformation intention; important prompts or instructions; intermediate outputs; revisions; and final alternatives.

## 7. SELECT

***Apply human judgement.***

Generation is not the end of the exercise.

Establish a small set of criteria that reflects what you were trying to achieve. Apply those criteria consistently to your alternatives, compare their strengths and weaknesses, and make a reasoned final selection.

Your choices should reveal **what human judgement adds to the generative process**.

### 7.1. Set criteria

Define a small number of criteria appropriate to your transformation intention.

Possible criteria include:

- **Addresses the intention:** Does the alternative respond meaningfully to the problem, opportunity, or transformation you defined?
- **Preserves important characteristics:** Does it retain important qualities of the original artefact where appropriate?
- **Maintains plausible logic:** Does it maintain reasonable spatial, structural, material, construction, or other relevant logic?
- **Produces meaningful change:** Is the transformation clear enough to matter rather than being merely cosmetic or arbitrary?
- **Offers something revealing:** Does the alternative suggest an unexpected, useful, provocative, or otherwise interesting direction?

You may modify or replace these criteria where another set is more appropriate to your investigation.

### 7.2. Evaluate consistently

For each collection:

- Apply the **same criteria** across the alternatives being compared.
- Identify important strengths, weaknesses, trade-offs, or failures.
- Avoid selecting an alternative only because it appears visually polished.
- Consider whether the output actually responds to the transformation intention and whether its underlying logic is convincing.

### 7.3. Select & explain

For each collection:

- Select approximately **3 final variations**.
- Briefly explain **why each variation was selected**.
- Identify **what changed** relative to the starting artefact.
- Explain **what the AI contributed** to the development of the alternative.
- Identify **what required human judgement**, correction, interpretation, or decision-making.
- Where useful, note promising ideas that emerged from unsuccessful alternatives.

Your final selection should demonstrate not only what the AI generated, but **how you evaluated, directed, and made decisions about what it generated**.
