## 1. Artefacts to Produce

The following are the working artefacts you should **produce and retain throughout the exercise**.

### GATHER

For each of your approximately **5 collections**, produce:

- Approximately **1–2 Context views or representations**.
- Approximately **2–3 System views or representations**.
- Approximately **3–5 Detail views or representations**.
- **One nested visual explanation per collection** showing how selected Detail view(s) relate to the System, and how the System relates to its wider Context.
- The **original photographs / visual material** and associated metadata where applicable.

Your nested visual explanation should make this relationship immediately legible:

**Context → System → Detail**

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### EXAMINE

For each collection, document:

- Your **initial interpretation** of the collection.
- Important **features or conditions** you noticed.
- Important **uncertainties, ambiguities, or missing information**.
- Conditions that appear worth testing with AI.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### IDENTIFY

Produce:

- Approximately **5 systematic questions** that can be tested across multiple collections.
- Approximately **1–2 collection-specific questions per collection**.
- Across your questions, include tests of:
  - **relationships / connectivity**;
  - **interpretation / reasoning**;
  - **uncertainty / missing information**.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### INTERROGATE

For the VLM tests you conduct, retain:

- The **image(s) / visual evidence** provided.
- The **question** asked.
- The model's **first response**.
- Important **follow-up questions and responses** from your interrogation.
- Selected comparisons using different combinations of visual evidence, where relevant.

For important tests, preserve the basic sequence:

**Image(s) → Question → First AI Response → Follow-up Interrogation**

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### EVALUATE

Produce:

- An **evaluation record / table** for your important VLM tests.
- A judgement of **Correct / Partially Correct / Incorrect**, where appropriate.
- Notes on important:
  - errors;
  - unsupported assumptions;
  - uncertainty;
  - corrections;
  - contradictions.
- A short set of **key findings** identifying recurring capabilities and limitations of the VLM.

For important examples, make this relationship clear:

**Image → Question → AI Response → Your Evaluation**

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### TRANSFORM

For at least **3 selected collections**, produce:

- A clear **transformation intention**.
- Approximately **10 variations per collection**.
- A record of important **prompts, intermediate outputs, corrections, and revisions**.
- A visible development from the **starting artefact → iterative transformations**.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### SELECT

For each of the **3 transformed collections**, produce:

- A small set of **evaluation criteria**.
- Approximately **3 selected final variations**.
- A brief explanation of:
  - **why each was selected**;
  - **what changed** from the starting artefact;
  - **what the AI contributed**;
  - **what required human judgement**.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

## 2. Final Submission

The final submission should **distil the larger body of work above** into a clear and concise account of:

**What you collected → what you tested → what you learned → what you transformed**

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### 2.1. Abstract

Include:

- A **memorable title**.
- What you investigated.
- How you tested the VLM.
- Your most important **finding(s)**.
- A brief statement on what you explored during **TRANSFORM**.

Keep the abstract concise. It should communicate the main argument or story of your investigation rather than list every task completed.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### 2.2. Slides

Prepare a **portrait-format visual sequence** organised around:

**Gather → Examine → Identify → Interrogate → Evaluate → Transform → Select**

Use the slides to **select and distil the most important evidence**, rather than document every artefact you produced.

Include:

- Selected **Context → System → Detail** nested visual explanations.
- Your most important systematic and collection-specific questions.
- Important VLM tests presented clearly as:

**Image(s) → Question → AI Response → Your Evaluation**

- Key findings about the VLM's **capabilities, limitations, and recurring behaviours**.
- Selected transformation sequences showing how ideas developed through iteration.
- Your final selected variations and the reasoning behind them.

Use **visual evidence and concise annotation** rather than long paragraphs.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### 2.3. Video

Prepare a **short video** that distils the main findings of your investigation.

Focus on:

- What you investigated.
- One or more important VLM tests.
- What those tests revealed.
- Your most important finding about the VLM's **capabilities or limitations**.
- How your approach changed when you moved from **testing the AI** during INTERROGATE to **working iteratively with the AI** during TRANSFORM.
- One final reflection on what you learned from the exercise.

The video should communicate the **main story of the investigation** rather than simply repeat the slides.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

### 2.4. Supporting Documentation

Retain and submit the underlying evidence used to construct your slides and video.

Include:

- Original visual collections.
- Context → System → Detail nested explanations.
- Available location / EXIF metadata.
- EXAMINE observations.
- Systematic and collection-specific questions.
- AI conversations and first responses.
- Follow-up interrogation records.
- Evaluation records / tables.
- Transformation prompts and intermediate outputs.
- Generated variations.
- Selected final variations.
- Final slides.

Think of the supporting documentation as the **evidence base** for the more selective story presented in your abstract, slides, and video.
