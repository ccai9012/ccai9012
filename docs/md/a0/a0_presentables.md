# _Presentables Checklist_

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

The **Presentables** are the outward-facing version of your investigation.

While the [**[Artefacts Checklist]**](a0_artefacts.html) documents the evidence and working material behind your project, the Presentables ask you to **select, edit, and distil** that larger body of work into a clear and engaging story.

A key objective is to practise **distillation**: deciding what matters most, what evidence best supports your findings, and how to communicate those ideas concisely to someone who has not followed your process.

Your final package consists of:

- **Abstract**
- **Slides**
- **Video — approximately 90 seconds to 2 minutes**

Selected work may also be shared through the course's **Instagram / public-facing channels**, so prepare these materials for an audience beyond the classroom.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

## (i) ABSTRACT

Prepare a concise abstract that introduces the main story of your investigation.

Include:

- A **memorable title**.
- What you investigated.
- How you tested the VLM.
- Your most important **finding or observation**.
- A brief indication of what you explored through **TRANSFORM**.

The abstract should **distil the project**, rather than list every task you completed.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

## (ii) SLIDES

Prepare a concise **portrait-format visual sequence** communicating the main progression of your investigation.

Use the slides to select the most important evidence from:

**GATHER → EXAMINE → IDENTIFY → INTERROGATE → EVALUATE → TRANSFORM → SELECT**

You do **not** need to reproduce everything contained in your submitted artefacts.

Instead, prioritise:

- Strong **Context → System → Detail** examples.
- Your most revealing questions and VLM tests.
- Clear examples of what the model did well or poorly.
- Comparisons that support your main findings.
- Selected transformation sequences.
- Your final selected outcomes.

Use **visual evidence, captions, diagrams, tables, and concise annotations** rather than long blocks of text.

The slides should make sense to someone encountering your project **for the first time**.

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

<hr>

<div style="height:1.5rem"></div>

## (iii) VIDEO

Prepare a short video of approximately **90 seconds to 2 minutes**.

The video should communicate the **main story** of your investigation rather than simply reproduce your slides.

Aim to communicate:

- What you investigated.
- One or more revealing VLM tests.
- What you discovered about the model's capabilities or limitations.
- How you moved from **testing the AI** to **working with the AI**.
- The most interesting transformed or selected outcomes.
- A concise final takeaway.

Think carefully about **pace, sequencing, visual hierarchy, and narration**. You will need to leave many things out — that is part of the exercise.
