Submission Components

As in the case study, give a short overview in the abstract and use the video to present the project. The report explains your methods and findings in more detail. Include supporting evidence as an appendix if it helps readers understand your results.

i. ABSTRACT

Introduce the problem and your team’s approach, then explain how the project addresses each of the three course dimensions.

• 300-word overall case description
    – Describe the problem, situate it in context, and explain why it matters.

• Three focused sections (max 200 words each):
    – Ethics, responsibility, and governance
    – Application mechanics and system pipeline
    – Data construction and associated risks

Show how the three dimensions connect in your project. For example, explain how a choice about the data affects the system’s outputs and what users can reasonably do with those outputs.

ii. REPORT

Write the report as an academic paper. Explain how you developed and tested your approach, and what the results showed. The report will be exported directly as slides for the course gallery, so you only need to prepare the report for the gallery.

• Submission format — Microsoft Word (.docx)
• Suggested Max 1,500 words
• Suggested Max 10 A4 pages (including figures, excluding references)

Suggested report structure

Adapt or combine the sections below as needed. Make the link between your methods, results, and conclusions clear.

• Introduction & Motivation
    – Define the problem and why it matters
    – Situate within context (e.g. built environment, sustainability)

• System Overview & Approach
    – Describe the overall workflow or pipeline
    – Clarify the role of AI

• Methods & Implementation
    – Explain where your data came from, how you generated any new data, and how you prepared it for use.
    – Describe the models and tools you used, and explain the technical decisions that shaped your approach.

• Results & Outputs
    – Use examples or visuals to show how the system behaved in your tests.     – Explain what those results support and where the evidence is limited.

• Analysis & Reflection
    – Explain how your choices about the system and its data affect how people can use it responsibly.

• Limitations & Future Directions
    – Identify constraints and possible improvements

• Conclusion
    – Summarise contribution and key insights

iii. VIDEO

Use the video to introduce your project to someone seeing it for the first time.

Submit the video separately by 00:00 on Wednesday, 2 December 2026 (midnight between 1 and 2 December, before class) using the [Final Project Video Submission Form].

You may use the same form to resubmit a revised video by 00:00 on Wednesday, 9 December 2026 (midnight between 8 and 9 December).

• Duration — Approximately 4 minutes
• Format — Vertical (portrait) video

Start with the central idea, show how the system or workflow works, and close with the findings or insights you want the audience to take away. Use visuals to help viewers follow the project’s main contribution.

iv. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE (optional)

Use the optional appendix for material that helps readers see how you reached a result or made a decision. Examples include the following.

• Code snippets
• Parameter sweeps
• Prompt comparisons
• Additional diagrams or tables
• Experimental results

What Your Submission Should Demonstrate

Give a complete account of your project at its current stage. Explain what you built or proposed, what you tested, and what you learned.

• Problem Framing

    – A clearly defined and bounded problem
    – A meaningful context and motivation

• AI-Assisted System

    – A coherent system, workflow, or design proposal
    – A clear role for AI within the project

• Implementation and Results

    – Demonstration of system behaviour through outputs
    – Evidence of testing, iteration, or evaluation

• Critical Integration

    – Reasoning across mechanisms, datasets, and responsibility
    – Awareness of limitations, risks, and implications

Expectations

Use your writing, visuals, and narration to guide readers and viewers through the same project. Make it easy to follow the decisions your team made, the reasons for those decisions, and what your evidence supports.

Grading Criteria

Use the grading criteria and rubric to review how clearly your submission shows the thinking behind your project.

In a nutshell

We are not grading:

  • completeness of the project
  • polished final results
  • advanced technical implementation

We are grading:

Criterion What It Means
Problem Framing Is the problem or opportunity clearly defined, bounded, and situated in context?
System Concept Is the proposed AI-assisted approach understandable, coherent, and plausibly aligned with the problem?
Process Thinking Do you demonstrate structured thinking about workflow, methods, and experimentation (i.e., how you will build, test, and iterate)?
Integration Do you reason critically about how mechanisms, datasets, and responsibility interact in the creation and use of the system?
Communication Are ideas communicated clearly through visuals and a coherent spoken narrative?

Rubric

Criterion Excellent Adequate Insufficient
Problem Framing Clear, specific, well-motivated problem with appropriate scope and constraints Problem identifiable but somewhat broad, vague, or under-scoped Problem unclear, overly broad, or poorly motivated
System Concept System/workflow clearly articulated with logical structure and clear role of AI Approach described but with gaps in structure, feasibility, or AI role clarity Approach confusing, inconsistent, or not clearly AI-assisted
Process Thinking Clear plan for methods and experimentation; shows iterative logic (what will be tested, how, and why) Some plan or early experiments shown, but methodology is underdeveloped Little evidence of planned or executed experimentation; process is ad hoc
Integration Thoughtful early synthesis of mechanisms + datasets + responsibility, with clear implications for design decisions Mentions these dimensions but connections are shallow or unclear Treats dimensions separately or ignores one or more; no critical reasoning
Communication Strong visual storytelling; minimal text; clear pacing; narrative makes feedback needs explicit Understandable but uneven; some text-heavy slides or unclear transitions Hard to follow; too dense; visuals do not support the narrative
